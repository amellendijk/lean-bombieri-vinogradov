\chapter{Type I sums: \texorpdfstring{$\Lambda^\sharp$}{Lambda sharp}}\label{ch:typeI}

The Type~I part $\Lambda^\sharp = \mu_{\le V} * \log - \Lambda_{\le U} * \mu_{\le V} * 1$ is a convolution of a
coefficient function of small support ($\mu_{\le V}$, respectively $\Lambda_{\le U} * \mu_{\le V}$) with one of
the smooth functions $\log$ and $1$. The discrepancy of such a convolution is a short sum of
discrepancies of the smooth factor at the points $y/k$ (\Cref{lem:Delta-convolution}), and those
are bounded pointwise, uniformly in the modulus, by Abel summation against the trivial bound
$|\Delta_1| \le 1$. This gives a pointwise bound of size $UV \log x$ for $\Delta_{\Lambda^\sharp}(y; q, a)$,
which is summed trivially over $q \le Q$.

There is one complication. What the conductor analysis of \Cref{ch:typeII} produces is not
$\Lambda^\sharp$ but its restriction $\Lambda^\sharp_r$ to integers coprime to $r$, and restriction turns the
smooth factor $\log$ into $\log_r$, which is neither smooth nor monotone. We therefore prove the
pointwise bound for $\Lambda^\sharp_r$, expanding $\log_r$ and $1_r$ as Möbius sums of dilations of
$\log$ and $1$; this costs a factor $\tau(r)$, which is harmless in both applications.

\begin{remark}
The factor $\tau(r)$ cannot be dropped from \Cref{prop:sharp-pointwise}: the discrepancy of $\log_r$ is
a signed combination of $2^{\omega(r)}$ residue-class counts with no cancellation in general, and the
Möbius expansion records this honestly. Downstream it is harmless, because $\tau$ is only ever
\emph{averaged} over the modulus (\Cref{prop:typeI-average}) or bounded by $2x^{1/4}$
(\Cref{lem:SW-flat-q}).
\end{remark}
